The Crown

The Crown is the supreme authority of the Realm. It unites executive power, sovereign law, military command, and the final authority of interpretation in matters of governance, succession, and public order.

Executive Authority

The monarch issues decrees, appoints officers, commands the military, and convenes the Great Council of the Realm.

Judicial Authority

The Crown may grant pardons, ratify judgments, supersede local legal practice, and settle disputes of special importance.

Religious Recognition

The sovereign is recognized as protector of the Faith, though the exact degree of religious authority varies by realm and confessional tradition.

Legitimacy

Legitimacy derives from lawful birth, the recognition of hereditary succession, the oath of fealty, and the acceptance of the people, nobility, and church.

Nobility & Feudal Structure

The Realm is organized according to a feudal and hereditary hierarchy in which lands, authority, and obligations are bound to bloodline, service, and oath.

Rank Power Responsibilities
Monarch Supreme sovereign authority Law, war, succession, diplomacy, tax and tribute
High Nobility Regions, castles, vassal courts Military levies, judicial oversight, local administration
Lower Nobility Landholding and minor governance Service to higher lord, dispute management, tax collection
Knights Military service and court honor Defense, oath-sworn service, arbitration
Commoners Property rights, labor, guild membership Taxes, service, protection, local duties

Vassalage

The lord grants land in exchange for fealty and service. The vassal owes military assistance, counsel, and tribute.

Right of Governance

Landholders may govern their territory, collect taxes, administer justice in lower courts, and exercise limited police authority.

Loss of Title

A noble may lose title through treason, failure in military service, rebellion, unlawful war, or conviction of serious crimes.

Administration

Government is exercised through multiple layers of office, each with limited competence and oversight.

Royal Council

The Council advises the Crown on war, taxation, succession disputes, and major reforms. It includes high nobles, judges, clerics, and senior administrators.

Chancellor

Responsible for royal records, state correspondence, and proclamation of law. Often acts as chief civil servant of the Crown.

Steward / Seneschal

Controls household administration, estates, revenues, and local enforcement within a noble's domain.

Sheriffs & Bailiffs

Serve as the Crown's local officers in enforcing law, collecting taxes, and exercising civil authority across a district.

Town Magistrates

Administer city affairs, regulate trade, maintain order, and serve as first instance in municipal cases.

Guild Wardens

Represent major economic bodies and mediate disputes among merchants, artisans, and local laborers.

Military & Security

Security of the realm is split between the royal army, local militias, and lesser sworn forces of the nobility.

Royal Guard

The Crown's personal military force, responsible for the sovereign's protection, important government buildings, and high-risk operations.

Household Troops

Retainers of the nobility, bound to service by lands, oaths, or wages. They enforce the law in large landed estates.

Militia

Commoners called to arms in defense of the realm, local shortages, or emergencies. Their authority is limited by rule, discipline, and call by lawful authority.

City Watch

Local peace officers responsible for watch, patrols, arrests, and public security in towns and market districts.

Military power exists under law. No private lord may mobilize armed forces beyond the authority granted by Crown, custom, or treaty without proper cause and lawful command.

Law & Justice

The legal structure of the Realm combines customary law, statutory decree, ecclesiastical principles, and the final authority of the Crown.

Local Courts

Resolve disputes over debt, property, local misconduct, and minor offenses. Often led by bailiffs, magistrates, or manorial judges.

Provincial Courts

Hears crimes of greater severity, disputes involving noble land, and appeals from lower courts. Department-level judges are often appointed by the Crown.

High Court

Composed of senior judges and royal officers, with authority over major criminal and constitutional cases.

Crown Court

Reserved for matters of treason, succession, extraordinary crime, and other disputes requiring direct royal intervention.

Succession & Inheritance

The Realm recognizes hereditary succession by bloodline, with certain rules to prevent disorder and civil war.

Issue Rule
Primary inheritance Legitimate children inherit in order of seniority and primogeniture unless custom or law says otherwise.
Illegitimate children Generally excluded from succession, but may receive support or land if recognized and favored by the Crown.
Disputed succession May be settled by parliament, council, church recognition, or trial by combat if lawful.
Forfeiture Titles may be forfeited if the holder commits treason, rebellion, or acts against the Crown.

Rights, Duties, and Privileges

Government is not absolute. The Crown and nobility exercise power under law, custom, and religious sanction, and no office is exempt from review by higher legal authority.

Rights of the Crown

To rule, tax, convene courts, command armies, appoint officers, and issue lawful decrees.

Rights of Nobles

To hold land, collect rents, maintain houses, grant charters, and oversee their own judicial domains.

Rights of the Common People

To inherit property, petition for justice, keep lawful possessions, and seek protection from arbitrary seizure and abuse.

Duties

All subjects owe loyalty, taxes, lawful service, and respect for the courts and sovereign authority.
