Foundations of Faith

The Faith of the Seven is the dominant religious tradition of the Realm. Its laws shape moral conduct, public ritual, sacred property, and the standards by which many legal disputes are judged.

The Seven

The Faith centers on seven aspects of divinity: the Father, the Mother, the Warrior, the Maiden, the Smith, the Crone, and the Stranger.

Religious Unity

The Crown, the church, and the common people are expected to maintain a shared moral order under the divine law of the Seven.

Ritual Structure

Prayer, fasting, vows, baptism, marriage rites, and funerary observance are central features of lawful life.

Core Religious Laws

Respect for the Sacred

Places of worship, relics, icons, and sacred objects must be protected from theft, vandalism, or desecration.

Marriage & Fidelity

Marriage is regarded as a sacred union and adultery, seduction, and illicit unions are serious moral and legal offenses.

Honesty Before God

Perjury, false oath, and deceit before the altar or in court are considered grave violations of divine and civic order.

Proper Worship

Public prayer, reverence for temples, respect for clergy, and proper observance of holy days are legally expected.

Charity & Mercy

Acts of generosity toward the poor, sick, and afflicted are promoted as a sacred duty and a moral obligation.

Clergy & Authority

Religious authority is exercised by clergy, abbots, septons, and recognized holy officers who interpret doctrine, enforce discipline, and guide the moral life of the populace.

High Septon

The spiritual leader of the Faith and senior interpreter of doctrine. May advise the Crown and settle questions of doctrine and faith.

Septons & Abbots

Supervise local worship, devotional life, charitable works, and religious education.

Ecclesiastical Courts

Handle cases of heresy, doctrine, marriage disputes, sacrilege, and clerical discipline.

Clerical Immunity

Ordained clergy may be subject to ecclesiastical law and discipline before civil courts in matters of doctrine and religious offense.

Sanctuary & Sacrilege

Sanctuary

Refuge within holy sites is protected under custom and sacred law. Violating sanctuary is a grave offense.

Sacrilege

Desecration of altars, relics, saints' icons, or hallowed grounds is punishable as a major religious crime.

Profanation of the Dead

Disturbing graves, desecrating bodies, or violating tombs is seen as an attack upon divine order and the moral life of the community.

Chartered Protection

Many temples and monasteries possess recognized legal authority to grant asylum or shelter under sacred protection.

Heresy & Apostasy

Religious deviation is not treated lightly. The faith distinguishes between ignorant error, deliberate denial, and active corruption of doctrine.

Offense Meaning Typical Remedy
Blasphemy Public contempt or mockery of the Faith or its sacred symbols Fines, imprisonment, or severe punishment if public and deliberate
Heresy Teaching or promoting unorthodox doctrine contrary to accepted interpretation Inquisition, exclusion, exile, or execution in severe cases
Apostasy Renunciation of the Faith or active rejection of its teachings Exile, imprisonment, loss of rights, or death under extreme circumstances
False Doctrine Spreading beliefs that mislead others or corrupt sacred order Judgment by church authority and civil penalties if required

Rituals & Ceremonies

Baptism

Recognized as the sign of entry into the community of faith and often required for lawful social standing.

Marriage Rites

Union by sacred vow is formal, often witnessed by clergy. The marriage contract may be recognized by the church and the Crown.

Funeral Rites

Burials must be performed with dignity and according to the Faith; desecration of the dead is a grave offense.

Fasting & Observance

Holy days, fasting periods, and acts of devotion are expected of believers and enforced by local moral custom.
