Royal Institutions

The Crown's authority is exercised through a network of formal and informal institutions. These bodies mediate between the sovereign's will and the practical administration of the realm.

The Great Council

Convened by the Crown to advise on matters of state, taxation, war, and succession. Includes high nobles, senior clergy, and chief magistrates. Not a legislature, but a body of counsel.

The Privy Council

Smaller, inner council of the Crown's closest advisors. Handles executive decisions, foreign policy, and sensitive matters of state security.

The Treasury

Responsible for Crown revenue, taxation, disbursement, and the management of royal lands and assets.

The Exchequer

The accounting authority that oversees tax collection, audits spending, and maintains records of Crown financial activity.

The Chancellery

Issues royal warrants, records decrees, maintains state correspondence, and preserves official records and law.

The War Council

Oversees military strategy, army organization, fortifications, and matters of defense and martial command.

Noble Hierarchy in Detail

Nobility is organized in tiers of rank and authority, each with defined privileges, duties, and territorial holdings. Rank is hereditary but may be granted, stripped, or modified by the Crown.

Rank Title Forms Territory Authority
Prince / Princess Royal Prince(ss), Royal Highness Principality or major duchy Near-royal authority over province; may hold own court and minor judgments
Duke / Duchess Duke, Your Grace Large territory (duchy), multiple holdings Judicial, military, and taxation authority within duchy; may delegate to lesser nobles
Marquis / Marchioness Marquis, Lord of the Marches March (border territory) Military and defensive authority; often granted autonomy in border disputes
Count / Countess Count, Your Lordship County or shire Local law, lower court, tax collection, and militia command
Baron / Baroness Baron, My Lord Barony (smaller holding) Limited jurisdiction, primarily manorial; answer to higher noble
Knight Sir / Dame, Knight Knighthood (modest holding or stipend) Local defense and law; ceremonial and military duties

The Royal Household

The Crown's personal retinue and administrative apparatus. Maintains the palace, manages royal affairs, and implements the sovereign's will in day-to-day governance.

Lord Chamberlain

Chief officer of the royal household; manages palace staff, ceremonies, and the sovereign's personal security and daily affairs.

Master of Ceremonies

Oversees court protocol, formal events, receptions, and the elaborate ritual that surrounds royal action.

Royal Guard Commander

Commands the armed force dedicated to the Crown's protection and the defense of the palace and state buildings.

Lord Steward

Manages royal estates, farm holdings, game forests, and the income and logistics of Crown properties.

Master of the Wardrobe

Maintains royal regalia, ceremonial garments, and the symbolic dress that marks the sovereign's station.

Court Physicians & Healers

Provide medical care and counsel on matters of health and wellness affecting the Crown.

Court Functions & Ceremony

The royal court is not merely a residence, but the center of government, law, diplomacy, and symbolic authority. Its rituals reinforce the Crown's legitimacy and the hierarchy of the realm.

Audience & Petition

Subjects may petition the Crown for favor, justice, or remedy. Audiences are formal, conducted before witnesses, and recorded.

Coronation & Investiture

The formal anointing and recognition of a new sovereign by church and council. Investiture of new nobles also occurs at court.

Trial by Combat

The Crown may authorize combat as a means of settling certain disputes or proving guilt. Court oversees the preparations and witnesses the outcome.

Royal Assent & Proclamation

Laws, decrees, and major decisions are announced at court, often with ceremony, to mark their official nature.

Feasts & State Dinners

Elaborate ceremonies that reinforce social hierarchy, bind the nobility through shared ritual, and display the Crown's power and wealth.

Marriage & Betrothal

Royal marriages are affairs of state, negotiated between houses and confirmed by the Crown. Often celebrated at court with great ceremony.

Regional Administration

The realm is divided into administrative regions, each with its own hierarchy of officials responsible to the Crown through the regional lord or governor.

Level Officer Responsibilities
Provincial / Regional Governor, Provincial Lord, or High Justice Overall administration, appointment of lower officers, major judicial appeals, military oversight
County / District Sheriff, Count, or Justice of the County Law enforcement, tax collection, local disputes, minor criminal matters
Town / Municipal Mayor, Magistrate, or Bailiff Local order, market regulation, municipal contracts, minor offenses
Manorial / Village Steward, Reeve, or Village Elder Day-to-day village affairs, dispute settlement, labor organization, manorial justice

Taxation & Revenue

The Crown's authority includes the right to tax and collect revenue. These funds support the military, administration, and the maintenance of law. Excessive taxation is a cause of rebellion and may be limited by custom.

Land Tax

A percentage or fixed sum owed annually by landholders to the Crown. Proportional to the value and size of holdings.

Merchant Tax

Duties on goods entering or leaving the realm, and fees paid by merchants for trading rights and market access.

Poll Tax

A tax levied on individuals, often during times of war or necessity. Unpopular and subject to resistance.

Feudal Dues

Payments owed by vassals to their lords, including military service, or payment in lieu of service.

Church Tithe

A portion of produce, income, or goods owed to the church. Often collected by the Crown and shared with religious authorities.

Tariffs & Market Fees

Revenue from the regulation of markets, licensing of trades, and fees for safe passage through Crown territory.

Diplomacy & Foreign Relations

The Crown conducts relations with neighboring realms through ambassadors, treaties, and formal exchange. Marriage alliances, trade agreements, and military pacts are common instruments of statecraft.

Ambassadors & Envoys

Officials sent to represent the Crown's interests abroad. Often of noble birth and bearing; afforded formal respect and immunity.

Treaties & Compacts

Formal agreements between realms, often sealed by oaths and witnessed by the church. Violations may trigger warfare or legal action.

Marriage Alliances

Betrothals between noble houses to cement political bonds, unite territories, or prevent conflict.

Trade Agreements

Negotiated terms for commerce, tariffs, and mercantile access to foreign markets.

Hostages & Pledges

High-ranking individuals held as surety for the performance of agreements or as security against betrayal.

Succession in Detail

The line of succession is the lifeblood of stability in the realm. Disputed succession can trigger civil war, and the rules governing inheritance are therefore sacred and closely observed.

Circumstance Rule
No direct heir Passes to the eldest sibling of the previous monarch, or their heir, unless the council selects another.
Multiple claimants Resolved by legitimacy, age, gender rules (varies by tradition), or by appeal to the Great Council or the church.
Female heirs In some realms permitted; in others excluded. When permitted, rank below male heirs of the same generation.
Bastards Generally excluded unless legitimized by the previous monarch or acknowledged by the Crown before death.
Regency If the heir is a minor, a regent (usually a senior family member or lord) rules on their behalf until maturity.
Abdication A monarch may surrender the crown to a chosen heir, though this is rare and must be witnessed formally.
Deposition A reigning monarch may be removed by the Great Council or the church if deemed unfit or a traitor. Extreme remedy.
