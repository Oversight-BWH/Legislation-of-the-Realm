Overview

Civil law governs relations among private persons, households, guilds, landholders, and merchants. It preserves order in trade, inheritance, marriage, labor, property, and daily obligations.

Purpose

To define rights, obligations, and remedies between private parties without requiring criminal prosecution.

Scope

Applies to disputes between citizens, merchants, landowners, laborers, and organizations, unless a matter falls under criminal or ecclesiastical jurisdiction.

Core Rights

Right to Property

Subjects may possess, inherit, transfer, rent, or sell lawful property subject to custom, lordship, and tax obligations.

Right to Contract

Private parties may enter voluntary agreements if they are lawful, informed, and not contrary to the Crown, faith, or public order.

Right to Petition

Any subject may seek redress from lawful authority through petition, complaint, or appeal to a higher court.

Right to Defense

Every person is entitled to formal hearing, legal representation where allowed, and fair consideration before judgment.

Right to Due Process

No one may be deprived of liberty, land, office, or rank without lawful cause, hearing, and proper judicial authority.

Property & Inheritance

Ownership of land and goods is a core civil right. The law recognizes both formal ownership and customary use rights.

Issue Rule
Land ownership Land is held subject to lordship, taxes, customary duties, and lawful transfer.
Inheritance Property passes by inheritance, will, marriage settlement, or hereditary custom.
Forced sale Property may be seized only by lawful judgment, tax authority, or other recognized legal process.
Boundary disputes Settled through survey, witness testimony, custom, and court order.

Contracts & Obligations

Voluntary Agreement

A valid contract requires meeting of minds, lawful purpose, and mutual consent. It may be written, witnessed, or established by customary practice.

Debt & Credit

Loans and debt obligations are enforceable by law and often secured by collateral, bonds, or guild assurance.

Service Contracts

Labor, apprenticeship, and service obligations are recognized if not contrary to law, faith, or public policy.

Fraudulent Contract

Any contract procured by deceit, coercion, or misrepresentation may be voided or enforced subject to equitable remedy.

Family & Domestic Law

Family relations are regulated by marriage law, inheritance custom, household authority, and religious norms.

Marriage

Marriage creates mutual rights and obligations, including legitimacy of children, shared property, and household authority.

Consent & Capacity

Marriage requires free consent, lawful status, and compliance with local custom. Forced marriage is voidable.

Divorce

Divorce may be granted on grounds such as abandonment, infidelity, abuse, or religious incompatibility depending on jurisdiction.

Guardianship

Children and dependents may be placed under lawful guardianship, especially where the parent is absent, dead, or disqualified.

Labor & Guilds

Employment

Workers and employers are bound by wage agreements, service duration, and customary obligations unless unlawfully coerced.

Apprenticeship

Apprentices are bound by contract, usually for a fixed term, with duties of training, work, and obedience.

Guild Regulation

Guilds set standards for craft quality, training, fees, and trade discipline, but may not violate Crown law or public order.

Wages & Fees

Payment terms may be enforced through local courts, guild tribunals, and subjects of custom and oath.

Dispute Resolution

Negotiation & Settlement

Many civil disputes are settled privately, by mediators, or by respected local elders before formal litigation.

Local Arbitration

Parties may agree to a neutral arbiter whose decision is binding if agreed in writing or by custom.

Court Remedy

Courts may award damages, restitution, injunctions, title restitution, or specific performance depending on the claim.

Equitable Relief

In some matters, the court may prevent unjust enrichment, enforce fairness, or compel a party to fulfill a prior obligation.
